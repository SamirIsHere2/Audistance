Hearing Test

Pure Tone Audiometry

A supplement to your audiologist's exam between visits — not a replacement for it — across six frequencies (250 Hz–8 kHz), followed by ten spoken words at varying volumes. Put on headphones in a quiet room, set a comfortable volume, and click the button the moment you hear each tone. When the word portion starts, we'll ask for one-time microphone access — words are spoken aloud, never shown on screen, so just say back what you hear. Full results appear together at the end.

Listen closely…
This pure tone audiometry check is a simple, uncalibrated self-screen meant to supplement — never replace — clinical care. Actual loudness depends on your device and headphone volume, so it can't verify true hearing thresholds the way calibrated audiometric equipment can, and speech recognition accuracy varies by browser, accent, and background noise. See a licensed audiologist for a full clinical diagnosis.
Extension

OtoCorrect, everywhere in Chrome

A companion browser extension that brings distance-based volume to any tab — not just this site. Same camera-driven idea as the Sound page, applied across your whole browser.

OtoCorrect Extend · In Development

Distance-based volume for Chrome

OtoCorrect Extend will watch your camera in the background and gently turn the volume of whatever's playing — YouTube, Spotify Web, a video call — up or down as you move closer to or farther from your screen. We're putting the finishing touches on it now.

Free · Manifest V3 · Works fully offline · Nothing from your camera ever leaves your device.

1

Follows you automatically

A lightweight offscreen camera loop estimates your distance roughly every 100ms and smoothly eases the volume to match — no need to touch a slider.

2

Works on almost any site

Covers plain <audio>/<video> elements as well as sites that generate sound through the Web Audio API, like games and browser-based synths.

3

Private by design

Camera frames are analyzed locally and never recorded or uploaded. The extension only ever adjusts playback volume inside your Chrome tabs.

How it works

Using the app, step by step

1

Open the app

OtoCorrect adds a small "oto" icon to your menu bar (Mac) or system tray (Windows) — no desktop window to manage, it just lives quietly up there.

2

Click it, hit Start Monitoring

A small menu drops down. Click Start Monitoring to begin the camera-to-volume loop in the background — nothing else on your screen changes.

3

Allow camera access

Your OS will ask permission the first time — allow it. Nothing from the camera is ever recorded or sent anywhere; it's only used locally to estimate distance.

4

Watch it update in real time

Open the live camera preview to see the detected face box plus the current distance and volume, updating continuously as you move — the same numbers driving your volume behind the scenes.
